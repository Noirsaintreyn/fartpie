## report_86 note (Nov 14 2026): what makes a flip big (context + supply + payoff)
Declared H1 (compressed pre-flip range better) and H2 (extended / post-blow-off flips worse) both rejected: direction is the OPPOSITE. Flips after range expansion (ATR10/ATR60 high) beat compressed ones, +1.2pp (2019-21) / +1.3pp (2022+), pooled t +2.1, small and post-hoc, medians worse; extension off the 60d low also tilts toward more huge moves (2.6% vs 0.6% huge, pooled), not significant. H3 (supply) is the strongest structure seen: huge-flip rate and mean fall monotonically with price and dollar volume in both eras (<$5 huge 3.5-16% vs 0.6-1.1% for $20+; <$1M dollar volume huge 3.6-6.7% vs 0.8-1.7% for $25M+). Caveats: tiny n for <$2 (81 / 213), survivorship bias is worst there, 30bp costs are unrealistic below ~$1M dollar volume. Payoff bootstrap: 2019-21 needs ~100+ flips/year for P(year>0) 88%, ~250 for 98%; 2022+ whole-universe mean is ~0 so P(year>0) stays ~50% at any K. Median year without its single best trade is negative below K=50. Move size looks supply/liquidity driven, not institution-driven.
